03 / HOW I LEAD

Direction is a
team practice.

Technical depth helps me understand the choices. Leadership means making those choices clear enough for others to act.

DEFINE THE FIT. MAKE THE WORK LEGIBLE.Drafted by your scroll ↓

01

Purpose. Choose what matters.

Start with the change we need to make, the people it serves, and the constraints that matter. A clear purpose gives a team something useful to decide against.

What would change this priority?

A useful priority has a reason and a boundary. I want to name the constraint or new evidence that would make us reconsider it.

02

People. Make ownership clear.

Connect the people closest to the problem. Make responsibilities, dependencies and decisions visible so expertise can turn into coordinated work.

Where does a decision get stuck?

Look at the handoff: who has the context, who owns the decision, and who needs to understand the tradeoff?

03

Systems. Build for the next person.

A good solution has to be understandable and repeatable. I look for the documentation, interfaces and working habits that help a team carry it forward.

Could someone else carry this forward?

Understanding should survive a handoff. Clear interfaces and written reasoning reduce reliance on any one person.

04

Feedback. Let reality answer.

Make assumptions testable. Observe what happens, invite challenge and use what we learn to change direction when the evidence calls for it.

What evidence would change our minds?

Define the observation before defending the solution. A small test can be more useful than another confident opinion.

03.2 / FROM INTENT TO DELIVERY

Make the sequence visible.

Agree on the problem. Align the people. Build in increments. Leave room to learn.

Define the problem → align ownership → build and test → review and adapt. Phases overlap as learning informs the work.
